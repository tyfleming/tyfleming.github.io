About

Imaging science, with an eye for patterns.

I’m an Imaging Science PhD student at Washington University in St. Louis. My work connects computational methods with questions about how the brain develops, changes, and moves between states.

01 / Background

From image analysis to neuroimaging.

Before my PhD work, I studied electrical and computer engineering at Marquette University and worked on deep learning methods for breast cancer imaging. That experience shaped my interest in methods that are technically careful and scientifically interpretable.

At WashU, I work with Muriah Wheelock’s neuroimaging lab on spatial patterns across development and degeneration, alongside work on fMRI sleep-stage decoding.
