Research / 02

Decoding sleep from brain activity.

Sleep matters for brain and physical health, but its changing whole-brain patterns are difficult to measure. I am developing an fMRI-based way to estimate Wake, N1, N2, and N3 with calibrated probabilities—so sleep state can be studied, tracked, and accounted for in brain imaging research.

Why it mattersSee the methodExplore probabilities
01 / Why it matters

Make brain state measurable.

EEG provides sleep-stage labels; fMRI shows activity across the brain. Connecting them gives researchers a way to ask how large-scale networks change as a person becomes drowsy, enters sleep, and moves between stages.

01

Interpret brain scans

Arousal changes can alter the fMRI signal and apparent connectivity. A window-level estimate could help imaging teams distinguish changes in vigilance from effects they attribute to disease, drugs, or interventions in resting-state studies.

02

Study transitions

Four probabilities preserve uncertainty when a window sits between stages. That makes it possible to examine changing network patterns without treating every transition as a certain, abrupt switch.

03

Build reliable research tools

Short windows, motion, and person-to-person variation make brain-state inference difficult. This project tests the quality control, participant-held-out validation, and calibrated uncertainty needed before estimates can be compared across people or studies.

Overall goal. Develop a trustworthy brain-state measurement layer for sleep neuroscience and for imaging studies where changes in vigilance affect interpretation. This is a research-stage method; broader use needs independent validation.

Background: NIH on sleep and health · simultaneous EEG–fMRI sleep data · arousal effects on fMRI.

02 / Method

From window to probabilities.

0130 frames · 62.4 s

A short window

Use the previous 30 fMRI frames (62.4 seconds) to make a prediction at the window’s endpoint.

02333 → 39

Cortical signals

For each of 13 networks, keep its average signal and two patterns of how its parcels differ from that average.

0339 × 39

Connectivity geometry

Measure which of the 39 signals change together, then stabilize the estimate from this short window.

04tangent space

Tangent features

Compare current and earlier connectivity patterns with training references, then reduce them to 64 numbers.

054 stages

Stage probabilities

Compare those 64 numbers with learned stage patterns and adjust the resulting probabilities for confidence.

062 branches

Complementary cycle branch

Also measure lagged ordering among the original parcel signals; this second branch produces four probabilities to blend with the first.

A volume is one fMRI snapshot; a parcel is one small cortical region. Each snapshot contains 333 parcel measurements, and 30 consecutive snapshots make one prediction window. N1, N2, and N3 are non-REM sleep stages.

The exact inputs, equations, and outputs are laid out below.

03 / Equations

Inside the decoder.

Both branches use the same past-only fMRI window. The first follows how large-scale signals change together; the second measures lagged patterns among parcels. Each returns probabilities for Wake, N1, N2, and N3, which are then blended.

01

Causal window

Take 30 consecutive fMRI snapshots ending at the moment being classified. “Causal” means the calculation uses no future measurements; it does not mean the model establishes a cause of sleep.

Input: one participant’s fMRI sequence and motion-validity mask. \(x_{u,j}\) is the signal in parcel \(j\) at volume \(u\), among 333 cortical parcels. A prediction at endpoint \(t\) uses only volumes through \(t\).

$$\begin{gathered}W_t=[x_{t-29},\ldots,x_t]\in\mathbb R^{30\times333}\\n_t=\sum_{u=t-29}^{t}\mathbf 1(\mathrm{FD}_u<0.2\ \mathrm{mm})\ge24\end{gathered}$$

TR is 2.08 seconds, so 30 volumes span 62.4 seconds; endpoints advance by five volumes (10.4 seconds). \(\mathrm{FD}_u\) is framewise displacement. The stage \(y_t\in\{\mathrm{Wake},\mathrm{N1},\mathrm{N2},\mathrm{N3}\}\) at the endpoint supervises training and evaluation, never inference. Transition windows are retained; no stage-purity filter is used. Output: one eligible window and its validity mask.

02

Network signals and residual modes

Each fMRI snapshot gives a measured signal for every cortical parcel. The atlas groups those 333 parcels into 13 networks; the Default network contains 41. For one network at one snapshot, average its parcel values, then subtract that same-snapshot average from every parcel in that network. The resulting differences are the residuals: which parcels are above or below their network’s shared level. They come directly from the parcel measurements; they are not prediction errors or discarded noise.

Input: the 333 parcel values at volume \(u\). For network \(g\), \(\mathcal P_g\) is its parcel set, \(m_{u,g}\) its same-volume average, and \(e_{u,g}\) its vector of parcel-wise residuals.

$$\begin{gathered}m_{u,g}=\frac1{|\mathcal P_g|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal P_g}x_{u,j}\\e_{u,g}=x_{u,\mathcal P_g}-m_{u,g}\mathbf1\end{gathered}$$

At every volume, the residuals across a network sum to zero. They preserve the within-network pattern even when the network average changes. To compress that pattern, principal component analysis (PCA) learns the two directions along which training residual patterns vary most. Only training participants are used. Each participant contributes 200 approximately evenly spaced motion-valid volumes so no participant dominates the fit.

$$\begin{gathered}\mu_g=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}\frac1{200}\sum_{u\in V_s}e_{s,u,g}\\K_g=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}\frac1{200}\sum_{u\in V_s}(e_{s,u,g}-\mu_g)(e_{s,u,g}-\mu_g)^\top\\U_g=[u_{g,1},u_{g,2}]=\operatorname{top2eig}(K_g),\quad a_{u,g}=U_g^\top(e_{u,g}-\mu_g)\in\mathbb R^2\end{gathered}$$

\(S\) is the set of accepted training participants; \(V_s\) contains participant \(s\)’s 200 sampled valid volumes. The training residual mean \(\mu_g\) is a separate, parcel-by-parcel reference, averaged first within participant and then across participants. It is different from the network mean \(m_{u,g}\), which is recomputed at every volume. A residual vector sums to zero across parcels, but one parcel can still be above its network average often enough to have a nonzero training reference.

\(K_g\) records which residuals vary together across training volumes. Its two leading directions form \(U_g\). For a new volume, subtract the fixed training reference \(\mu_g\) from its residual vector and project onto those two directions. The result \(a_{u,g}\) is two scores for that volume, not a whole-window summary. A positive or negative score indicates how strongly the parcel pattern aligns with or opposes a learned direction. The atlas community named “None” is included among the 13. For the Default community, \(U_{\mathrm{Default}}\in\mathbb R^{41\times2}\): one row for each of its 41 parcels and one column for each of two residual directions. The 41 counts parcels, not volumes or participants.

$$\begin{gathered}f_u=[m_{u,1:13},a_{u,1}^{\top},\ldots,a_{u,13}^{\top}]\in\mathbb R^{39}\\b_{u,q}=\frac{f_{u,q}-\bar f_q}{\sqrt{\max(\sigma_q^2,10^{-12})}},\qquad39=13+13(2)\end{gathered}$$

Each network now supplies three signals per volume: its shared mean and two within-network residual scores. Across 13 networks, that makes 39. Here \(q=1,\ldots,39\) indexes those concatenated signals; \(\bar f_q\) and \(\sigma_q^2\) are their training-fold mean and variance on the same sampled valid volumes. This second standardization covers all 39 channels, including the network means. \(B_t=[b_{t-29},\ldots,b_t]^\top\) stacks the standardized per-volume vectors. The frozen transform is applied to every volume; censored volumes are not interpolated, and only valid rows enter covariance. Output: a 30 × 39 standardized signal window \(B_t\).

03

Shrinkage correlation matrix

The 39 columns now describe network averages and within-network patterns over time. A 39 × 39 matrix summarizes which pairs rise and fall together inside the window. With only 24–30 usable snapshots, the raw estimate is fragile; shrinkage pulls it toward a diagonal baseline to make it stable.

Input: \(Y_t\in\mathbb R^{n_t\times39}\), the motion-valid rows of \(B_t\), with \(24\le n_t\le30\). There are fewer rows than channels, making an ordinary sample covariance unstable. Ledoit–Wolf estimates a shrinkage covariance.

$$\begin{gathered}\widehat\Sigma_t=(1-\lambda_t)S_t+\lambda_t\mu_t I,\qquad\mu_t=\operatorname{tr}(S_t)/39\\D_t=\operatorname{diag}(\widehat\Sigma_t),\qquad C_t=D_t^{-1/2}\widehat\Sigma_tD_t^{-1/2}\in\mathcal S_{++}^{39}\end{gathered}$$

\(S_t\) is covariance across valid rows, \(\lambda_t\) its automatically estimated shrinkage fraction, \(I\) the identity, and \(D_t\) the resulting variances. Variance normalization yields a correlation-like matrix. The implementation symmetrizes it, floors eigenvalues at \(10^{-6}\max(\lambda_{\max},1)\), and restores a unit diagonal. \(\mathcal S_{++}^{39}\) denotes symmetric positive-definite 39 × 39 matrices. Output: a stabilized connectivity state \(C_t\).

04

Causal history in tangent space

Connectivity is a matrix, so the model compares each matrix with a training-derived reference and turns the difference into ordinary numerical features. It does this for the current window and two earlier windows, preserving a short history without using future data.

Input: the current correlation matrix and matrices nominally 25 and 50 volumes earlier (52 and 104 seconds). These are volume lags, not counts of windows. If an exact lag endpoint is ineligible, the latest eligible earlier endpoint is used; at the beginning of a scan the lookup is clamped to its first eligible window.

$$\begin{gathered}G_\ell=\exp\!\left(\frac1{N_\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{N_\ell}\log C_{i,\ell}^{\mathrm{train}}\right),\quad\ell\in\{0,25,50\}\\T_{t,\ell}=\log\!\left(G_\ell^{-1/2}C_{t,\ell}G_\ell^{-1/2}\right)\end{gathered}$$

\(N_\ell\) counts training matrices at lag \(\ell\); \(G_\ell\) is their frozen log-Euclidean reference. The inverse square root compares a window’s matrix with that reference, and the matrix logarithm acts on eigenvalues to give a symmetric tangent matrix \(T_{t,\ell}\).

$$\begin{gathered}\operatorname{svec}(T)=[\operatorname{diag}(T),\sqrt2\,\operatorname{upper}(T)]\in\mathbb R^{780}\\v_t=[\sqrt{4/7}\operatorname{svec}(T_{t,0}),\sqrt{2/7}\operatorname{svec}(T_{t,25}),\sqrt{1/7}\operatorname{svec}(T_{t,50})]\in\mathbb R^{2340}\end{gathered}$$

\(\operatorname{upper}\) contains the 741 strictly upper-triangle entries; the diagonal adds 39. The square-root factors give the three lags a 4:2:1 energy weighting. Each of the 2,340 coordinates is clipped to its training 0.5th–99.5th percentiles; training-fitted randomized PCA retains 64 whitened components. Output: \(z_t\in\mathbb R^{64}\). References, clipping limits, axes, and whitening scales exclude held-out participants.

05

Tangent-stage decoder

From the 64 connectivity features, a classifier compares the window with patterns learned for the four stages. Its initial probabilities are then temperature-calibrated: the values become less or more confident without changing which stage has the largest value.

Input: the 64 whitened tangent features \(z_t\). Shrinkage linear discriminant analysis compares \(z_t\) with four training-stage means under one automatically shrunk within-stage covariance and equal class priors.

$$\begin{gathered}\delta_k(z)=z^\top\widetilde\Sigma^{-1}\mu_k-\frac12\mu_k^\top\widetilde\Sigma^{-1}\mu_k+\log\frac14\\p_{\mathrm{raw},k}=\frac{e^{\delta_k}}{\sum_j e^{\delta_j}},\qquad p_{\mathrm{tan},k}=\frac{p_{\mathrm{raw},k}^{1/T_{\mathrm{tan}}}}{\sum_jp_{\mathrm{raw},j}^{1/T_{\mathrm{tan}}}}\end{gathered}$$

\(k\) indexes Wake, N1, N2, and N3; \(\mu_k\) is the stage-\(k\) mean, and \(\widetilde\Sigma\) is the shared covariance. The discriminant describes the scikit-learn estimator conceptually. \(T_{\mathrm{tan}}>0\) is chosen by minimizing multiclass log loss on participant-grouped training predictions; the temperature transform is literal. No participant-specific signal alignment was selected. Output: four tangent-branch probabilities summing to one.

06

Directed-cycle decoder

This second branch starts again from the original parcel signals. It compares early and late activity on pairs of nearby parcels, then summarizes how the resulting signed flows form paths and loops on a fixed atlas graph. “Directed” describes timing asymmetry in the calculation; it does not establish neural causation.

Input: the original 30 × 333 parcel window, not the 39 compressed signals. A fixed symmetrized six-nearest-neighbor atlas graph has 333 nodes, 1,152 undirected edges, and 986 filled triangles. Each edge receives a fixed orientation for signed-flow bookkeeping. Graph topology comes from atlas coordinates, not stage labels.

At within-window lags \(\tau\in\{1,2,3\}\) volumes, use only early–late pairs whose two frames are motion-valid. For each parcel and each side of a pair, \(\widetilde x\) means its series centered and unit-normalized across eligible pairs. For an oriented edge \(i\to j\), this difference measures lagged asymmetry, not proof of neural causation.

$$f_{ij}^{(\tau)}=\sum_u\widetilde x_{u,i}^{\mathrm{early}}\widetilde x_{u,j}^{\mathrm{late}}-\sum_u\widetilde x_{u,j}^{\mathrm{early}}\widetilde x_{u,i}^{\mathrm{late}}$$

Each lag yields a 1,152-edge flow \(f^{(\tau)}\). \(B_1\in\mathbb R^{333\times1152}\) is the node–edge incidence matrix and \(B_2\in\mathbb R^{1152\times986}\) the edge–triangle incidence matrix. The graph Hodge decomposition separates source-to-sink flow, triangle circulation, and circulation around unfilled loops:

$$f=B_1^\top\phi+B_2\psi+f_H,\qquad f_H=HH^\top f$$

\(\phi\) is a node potential, \(\psi\) a triangle potential, \(f_H\) the harmonic part, and \(H\) an orthonormal 75-column basis of the graph Laplacian’s harmonic null space. \(Q_G\) and \(Q_C\) are orthonormal bases for gradient and curl flows. Each lag contributes five scalars:

$$E(f)=\left[\|f\|_2,\|H^\top f\|_2,\frac{\|H^\top f\|_2^2}{\|f\|_2^2},\frac{\|Q_G^\top f\|_2^2}{\|f\|_2^2},\frac{\|Q_C^\top f\|_2^2}{\|f\|_2^2}\right]$$

These are total magnitude, harmonic magnitude, and harmonic, gradient, and curl energy shares. Three lags give 15 values. Five more come from the pooled flow \(\bar f_t=(4f_t^{(1)}+2f_t^{(2)}+f_t^{(3)})/7\).

$$\begin{gathered}\eta_t=H^\top\bar f_t,\qquad\Delta\eta_t=\eta_t-\eta_{t^-}\\d_t=\|\Delta\eta_t\|_2,\quad c_t=\frac{\eta_t^\top\eta_{t^-}}{\|\eta_t\|_2\|\eta_{t^-}\|_2},\quad\theta_t=\arccos(c_t)\end{gathered}$$

\(t^-\) is the previous eligible window for the same participant, even across a motion-related gap. \(\eta_t\) holds the 75 pooled harmonic coordinates. Step norm \(d_t\), direction cosine \(c_t\), and angle \(\theta_t\) complete the 23 features. The 75 raw harmonic coordinates and their coordinate-wise changes are not direct classifier inputs; these three summaries use them indirectly.

$$\begin{gathered}p_{\mathrm{cycle,raw},k}=\frac{\exp(\beta_k^\top e+b_k)}{\sum_j\exp(\beta_j^\top e+b_j)},\qquad e\in\mathbb R^{23}\\p_{\mathrm{cycle},k}=\frac{p_{\mathrm{cycle,raw},k}^{1/T_{\mathrm{cycle}}}}{\sum_jp_{\mathrm{cycle,raw},j}^{1/T_{\mathrm{cycle}}}}\end{gathered}$$

\(\beta_k\) and \(b_k\) are learned class coefficients and intercepts. The 23 features are standardized on training data, then used by class-balanced multinomial logistic regression with L2 regularization. Supervised fitting uses endpoints ten volumes apart; inference covers the full five-volume grid. A positive cycle temperature \(T_{\mathrm{cycle}}\) is fitted on training-fold predictions before blending. Output: four calibrated cycle-branch probabilities.

07

Blend and calibrate

The two branches emphasize different information from the same window. A weight learned on training data mixes their four-stage probability vectors, then a final temperature adjusts confidence in the mixture.

Input: two four-stage probability vectors. The cycle weight \(\alpha\) is chosen within each outer-training fold from \(\{0,.01,.02,.05,.10,.20,.30\}\). Cross-fitted training predictions are ranked by macro-F1, balanced accuracy, class-balanced log loss, then smaller \(\alpha\). A final temperature is fitted after mixing.

$$\begin{gathered}q_{t,k}=(1-\alpha)p_{\mathrm{tan},t,k}+\alpha p_{\mathrm{cycle},t,k}\\p_{t,k}=\frac{q_{t,k}^{1/T_{\mathrm{final}}}}{\sum_jq_{t,j}^{1/T_{\mathrm{final}}}}\end{gathered}$$

\(q_{t,k}\) is the pre-temperature mixture for stage \(k\) at endpoint \(t\), and \(T_{\mathrm{final}}>0\) is the blend temperature. Output: \(p_t=(p_{t,\mathrm{Wake}},p_{t,\mathrm{N1}},p_{t,\mathrm{N2}},p_{t,\mathrm{N3}})\), whose nonnegative entries sum to one. Its largest entry is the predicted stage; the vector retains uncertainty.

08

Simplex projection

The tetrahedron is a way to display the four final probabilities as one point. It does not change the prediction. A point near a stage’s corner assigns more probability to that stage; a point nearer the center is less decisive.

Input: the final four probabilities. Each stage is one vertex of a regular tetrahedron. The point inside it is their probability-weighted average.

$$\begin{gathered} r=\sum_{k=1}^{4}p_kv_k \\ v_{\mathrm{Wake}}=(1,1,1),\qquad v_{\mathrm{N1}}=(1,-1,-1) \\ v_{\mathrm{N2}}=(-1,1,-1),\qquad v_{\mathrm{N3}}=(-1,-1,1)\end{gathered}$$

The view rotates and projects \(r\) to two dimensions. Rotation changes the viewpoint, never the probabilities. Output: the plotted point and a four-value text readout.

The interactive simplex below shows this projection for held-out predictions and provides a text readout for each selected window.

The residual directions, scaling, tangent references, clipping limits, PCA, classifiers, and temperatures exclude held-out subjects. The unsupervised tangent transforms are fitted on the complete outer-training fold before its grouped calibration splits. In this exploratory implementation, blend-weight selection used calibrated tangent probabilities and uncalibrated cycle probabilities; held-out blending used calibrated outputs from both branches.
