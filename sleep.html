Research / 02

Decoding sleep from brain activity.

Instead of asking a model for only one stage label, I study how short windows of fMRI activity can yield a probability for each of four states: Wake, N1, N2, and N3.

01 / Question

A state estimate with uncertainty.

Sleep changes continuously, while stage labels are discrete. A useful decoder should show its uncertainty, especially near transitions between stages.

The workflow below follows one causal fMRI window from measured signals to a four-number probability vector. Its numbered quantities describe the method; the simplex examples below remain illustrative.

Technical notes on the decoding pipeline

Window and representation

A trailing 30-frame window spans 62.4 seconds at a 2.08-second repetition time. Signals from the Gordon-333 cortical parcellation are reduced to 39 fold-fitted signals: 13 network means and 26 residual modes.

Geometry to classifier

Each eligible window yields a regularized 39 × 39 connectivity matrix. Current and past matrices provide causal context. Training-fold reference geometry maps them into tangent-space features; compact components feed a shrinkage linear discriminant model.

Probability calibration and complementary branch

Stage scores are temperature calibrated. A separate ridge branch summarizes cycle-related directed interactions; a nested blend can combine the branches. Fitting and calibration use training subjects, leaving held-out subjects out of those steps.
